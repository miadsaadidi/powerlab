Laboratory Procedure, Mathematical Formulations, and Simulation Workbench

1. Pedagogical Learning Objectives

Upon completion of this laboratory module, students will be able to:

  • Formulate solar declination (δ), hour angle (ω), and zenith angle (θz) for any geographic latitude and day of year (n).
  • Contrast isotropic transposition models (Liu-Jordan) against anisotropic formulations (Perez 1990) accounting for circumsolar and horizon brightening bands.
  • Utilize open-access computational simulation engines to determine seasonal and annual optimal tilt angles for fixed-tilt photovoltaic arrays.
  • Quantify geometric cosine losses and clipping penalties resulting from non-optimal fixed array orientations.

2. Theoretical Background & Mathematical Physics

2.1 Solar Declination and Geometric Incidence

The angle of solar declination δ represents the angular tilt of Earth's rotational axis relative to the ecliptic plane, modeled by Cooper (1969):

δ = 23.45° · sin((360 / 365) · (284 + n))

Where n is the day of the year (1 ≤ n ≤ 365).

2.2 Transposition from Horizontal to Plane of Array (POA)

Total incident solar radiation on a tilted collector surface (I_POA) comprises direct beam (I_b), sky diffuse (I_d), and ground-reflected (I_g) components:

I_POA = I_b,POA + I_d,POA + I_g,POA

Under anisotropic sky conditions, diffuse circumsolar irradiance is concentrated around the solar disk rather than uniformly distributed across the celestial hemisphere.

3. Laboratory Apparatus & Simulation Instrument

Students will utilize the deterministic simulation suite developed by the PowerLab Applied Energy Modeling Research Initiative to simulate geographic POA irradiance and energy yield without requiring local software compilation:

  • Interactive Laboratory Workbench: PowerLab Solar Panel Tilt & Output Simulation Engine
  • Reference Standard Test Conditions (STC): 1000 W/m², 25°C cell temperature, AM 1.5G spectrum.

4. Experimental Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Step 1: Station Configuration — Open the PowerLab Solar Panel Tilt & Output Simulation Engine. Enter your laboratory test latitude (e.g., 34.05° N for Los Angeles or 40.71° N for New York).
  2. Step 2: Baseline Measurement (Latitude Tilt) — Set array tilt equal to geographic latitude (β = Φ). Record the modeled annual Peak Sun Hours (PSH) and annual energy production (kWh/kWp).
  3. Step 3: Winter Optimization — Adjust array tilt to β = Φ + 15°. Record December and January POA irradiance and daily PSH values.
  4. Step 4: Summer Optimization — Adjust array tilt to β = Φ - 15°. Record June and July POA irradiance and daily PSH values.
  5. Step 5: Horizontal Collector Baseline — Set tilt to 0° (horizontal collector) and quantify the annual geometric insolation penalty.

6. Post-Lab Analytical Assessment

  1. Explain why an array tilted at β = Φ - 15° maximizes summer kilowatt-hour generation but results in a lower total annual economic yield in regions with flat year-round electricity tariffs.
  2. In high-albedo environments (ground snow cover, albedo = 0.65), how does increasing the tilt angle beyond latitude impact the ground-reflected component I_g,POA?
  3. Calculate the percentage difference in annual kWh/kWp between the fixed latitude tilt and the seasonal optimum for your assigned latitude.
